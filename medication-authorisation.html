Portfolio  /  Medication authorisation
Real professional experience  •  Generalised reconstruction

Making a complex process easier to manage.

A practical Lean Six Sigma improvement project focused on making upcoming medication authorisation work more visible, hand-offs clearer and follow-up easier across teams.

What this case study is

Based on professional work. This public narrative reconstructs the methods and lessons at a high level. It does not reproduce any patient information, sponsor details, financial figures, internal reports, systems or proprietary documentation.

Evidence boundary. Outcomes below are qualitative descriptions from follow-up reviews, not independently audited figures or approved performance claims.

01  /  Understand the problem

Make the next action visible.

In a busy healthcare setting, upcoming authorisation requests were not always visible far enough ahead. Information moved between teams and staff sometimes had to chase details close to the point of action.

The aim was not to add another tracker for its own sake. It was to understand where clarity was being lost, support colleagues with different priorities and make ownership and follow-up more consistent.

ChallengeLimited forward visibility and inconsistent cross-team follow-up
ContributionLed the investigation, mapped the workflow and coordinated improvement discussions
ApproachPractical improvement methods combined with stakeholder feedback
02  /  From questions to action

Understand first. Improve second.

I worked through the problem systematically, using familiar Lean Six Sigma tools where they could genuinely clarify the work.

01 / DEFINE

Listen

Gather perspectives from the teams involved and frame the problem before suggesting a solution.

02 / DEFINE

Map

Use a Project Charter, SIPOC and current-state process mapping to identify hand-offs and information needs.

03 / ANALYSE

Investigate

Use Fishbone themes and 5 Whys to challenge assumptions about late clarification and escalation.

04 / IMPROVE

Clarify ownership

Discuss responsibilities through RACI and define a clearer owner, next action and escalation route.

05 / IMPROVE

Make it usable

Support a shared view of outstanding work with simpler written guidance and more consistent follow-up.

06 / CONTROL

Review and adjust

Use post-implementation feedback and exception reviews to identify what still needs attention.

03  /  Outcomes and reflection

Better processes need to work day to day.

Before · Generalised

Reactive follow-up

Work became visible later, information crossed multiple teams and clarification sometimes happened close to deadlines.

After · Generalised

Clearer next steps

Greater forward visibility, a clearer owner and next action, more consistent guidance and earlier exception follow-up.

VisibilityUpcoming and outstanding work became easier to review.
OwnershipHand-offs and escalation paths were clearer across teams.
ConsistencyShared tracking and written guidance helped reduce variation.

Follow-up reviews described fewer missing authorisations and better visibility of outstanding items. I have deliberately omitted operational baselines and financial figures rather than presenting unverifiable public metrics.

What I learned: Different teams work under different pressures. Good process improvement depends on listening carefully, making responsibilities understandable and designing something colleagues can maintain.

What I would refine: Agree baseline definitions, reporting periods and exclusions earlier, so that later reviews are easier to compare.
